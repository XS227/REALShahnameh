Built 2026-07-11 by web research, cross-checked against at least one source

## 1. Composition history

- Ferdowsi began writing the Shahnameh around **977 CE**, initially under
  Samanid patronage (Prince Mansur, supported by the Samanid court's broader
  sponsorship of Persian literature under Nasr II). A first version was
  substantially complete by **994–995**. [Wikipedia: Ferdowsi]
- The Samanids fell to the Ghaznavid Turks in the late 990s; Ferdowsi
  revised the poem to include praise of the new ruler, Sultan **Mahmud of
  Ghazna**, and dedicated the completed work to him. [Wikipedia: Ferdowsi]
- Completion date: **8 March 1010 CE**, per Ferdowsi's own age references
  within the text. **Verified**, consistent across Wikipedia (Shahnameh)
  and Wikipedia (Ferdowsi). This is the figure already used in the
  numerologist.setai.no article ("Hva avslører tallene i Shahnameh?") —
  confirmed correct.
- Composition span: 1010 − 977 = **33 years**. **Verified**, stated
  directly by Wikipedia ("took approximately 33 years to complete").
- The famous "Mahmud paid in silver, not the promised gold" legend:
  Mahmud is said to have promised one gold dinar per couplet but sent
  20,000 silver dirhams instead of 60,000 gold coins; Ferdowsi allegedly
  gave the money away in anger. Mahmud's gold reportedly arrived only as
  Ferdowsi's funeral procession left Tus. **Disputed** — scholars debate
  whether this account is historically accurate or a later legend built
  around the patronage relationship. Good narrative material, bad thing to
  cite as established fact. [Wikipedia: Ferdowsi]
- Purpose/legacy: the Shahnameh is widely credited with helping preserve
  New Persian as a literary language and linking pre-Islamic Iranian
  identity to the post-Islamic era — this is the throughline for the "33 as
  master teacher" framing already used in the numerology article.
  [Wikipedia: Ferdowsi]

## 2. Ferdowsi's biography — dates are genuinely contested

- **Birth:** most widely cited year is **940 CE** (329 AH), based on
  Ferdowsi's own statement that he was 71 at the poem's completion in 1010.
  Alternative scholarly proposals exist: Nöldeke argued for c. 931–932,
  Homayounfarrokh for c. 925–926. **Disputed**, but 940 is the mainstream
  figure and safe to use as the primary claim with a light caveat.
- **Death:** genuinely unresolved in the scholarship — sources place it
  anywhere from **1020 to 1026 CE**. Two competing traditions: one favors
  ~1020 based on age calculations (Ferdowsi would have been ~80–82); the
  other favors **1025**, based on an anecdote from Neẓāmī ʿArūżī (a later
  biographer) about Ferdowsi's burial. **Disputed — do not present either
  1020 or 1025 as settled fact.** If a specific year is needed for a piece,
  say "traditionally placed around 1020–1025" rather than picking one.
  [Encyclopaedia Iranica; multiple secondary sources cross-checked]

## 3. Structure of the Shahnameh

Three traditional divisions, per Wikipedia and corroborated by the
Fitzwilliam Museum's Shahnameh exhibit materials:

| Age | Scope | Share of text |
|---|---|---|
| **Mythical** | Creation myths through Manuchehr; kings Keyumars, Hushang, Tahmuras, Jamshid, Zahhak, Fereydun | ~2,100 verses, ~4% of the work |
| **Heroic / Legendary** | Manuchehr through Alexander the Great's conquest; the Kayanian dynasty, Rostam and the other great heroes | ~two-thirds of the text |
| **Historical** | Brief Parthian/Ashkanian period, then the Sasanian dynasty in detail, ending with the Arab conquest of Iran in the 7th century | remainder |

Scale: some 50,000 distichs (couplets), ~990 chapters, 62 stories, covering
roughly **50 monarchs (47 kings, 3 queens)** across nine traditional
"volumes." [Wikipedia: Shahnameh; secondary corroboration via search]

The mythical age is explicitly Zoroastrian in its cosmogony — Ferdowsi does
not attempt to reconcile it with the Islamic creation account, notable
given when he was writing. [Wikipedia: Shahnameh]

The Sasanian section (historical age) is described by multiple sources as
"related with a good deal of accuracy" compared to external evidence
(numismatic, epigraphic, and Greco-Roman/Armenian/Islamic historical
sources) — i.e. this is the part of the poem where Ferdowsi's kings and
dates correspond most closely to real, independently verifiable history,
unlike the legendary reign-lengths in the mythical/heroic ages. **This
distinction matters for numerology pieces**: legendary-age numbers (700,
1000, 500 years, etc.) are Ferdowsi's own narrative/symbolic choices,
appropriate for the "what does the poem's own numerology say" framing
already used; Sasanian-age numbers are closer to actual historical dates
and should be framed differently if used (verifiable history, not poetic
symbolism).

## 4. Manuscript tradition

- **Florence Shahnameh (1217)** — oldest known surviving manuscript.
- **Great Mongol / "Demotte" Shahnameh** — produced under Ilkhanid rule,
  celebrated for its illustrations.
- **Baysunghuri Shahnameh (1430)** — preserved intact at Golestan Palace,
  Tehran; inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2007.
- First complete manuscript overall is dated **1276**.
- Regional manuscript styles are noted as distinct: "Tabrizi grandeur"
  versus "Shirazi delicacy." Some manuscripts include prologues or glosses
  not in others — a reminder that not every printed edition agrees
  word-for-word, which is part of why individual reign-year figures should
  always be sourced rather than assumed uniform across editions.
  [Wikipedia: Shahnameh]

## 7. Historical age — Sasanian dynasty (real dates, not legendary)

- The Sasanian Empire's real historical span is **224–651 CE**: founded by
  Ardashir I after defeating the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, in 224;
  ended with Yazdgerd III's defeat and the Arab conquest, completing by
  651. **Verified**, standard historical dating, not specific to the
  Shahnameh.
- Ferdowsi's account of this dynasty is repeatedly described in scholarship
  as historically accurate relative to independent evidence (coinage, rock
  inscriptions, Greco-Roman/Armenian/Islamic sources) — a sharp contrast to
  the symbolic, often-round numbers of the mythical/legendary ages (700,
  1000, 500 years, etc.).
- This pass did not compile a full Sasanian king-by-king reign-length table
  cross-checked against the Shahnameh's specific figures — flagged as a gap
  for a future research pass if a numerology piece specifically about the
  historically-grounded section is wanted. The interesting numerological
  angle here would be different in kind: real historical reign lengths
  rather than legendary/symbolic ones.

## 8. Prior "experiments" on the Shahnameh's numbers/timeline

**No dedicated numerological or reign-length-focused computational study of
the Shahnameh was found in this research pass.** Digital humanities work on
Persian poetry generally exists (e.g. a 2025 arXiv project on computational
analysis of Forough Farrokhzad's poetry, aimed at building public Persian
NLP datasets/models) but nothing found specifically analyzes Shahnameh's
king list, reign-year numerology, or internal chronology the way the
numerologist.setai.no article does. Broader digital-humanities method
categories that exist and could be adapted (verse-count/metrical analysis,
lexical/thematic pattern mining, network analysis of character
relationships) are documented in general digital-humanities literature, not
applied to the Shahnameh specifically in anything surfaced here.

**Implication for future content:** the numerological reading of
Shahnameh's reign-lengths is, as far as this research found, a genuinely
original angle for numerologist.setai.no — not a rehash of an existing
study. Worth stating that explicitly in future pieces rather than implying
prior art exists.
